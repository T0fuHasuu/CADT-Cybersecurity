Implementing NIST 2.0 CSF Framework



Enterprise Level



AP1 : Mission and Priorities expressed, Risk appetite defined

AP2 : Risk appetite interpreted, Risk tolerance defined

AP3 : Application of Risk Strategy



System Level



AP4 : Risk Assessment Conducted, Risk Response applied, Residual risk reflected in System Level CSRRs

AP5 : Risk register normalization/aggregation into org level CSRRs risk result reported feedback to refine risk tolerance

AP6 : Enterprise risk result ( including Enterprise CSRRR ) inform the enterprise risk register (ERR) and enterprise risk profile (ERP) support risk appetite refinement and improve enterprise risk decisions





##### How to Implement



1. **Understanding The Framework**

* Learn the six core of functions: Govern, Identity, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover
* Understand that these function represent the full lifecycle of managing cyber risk
* Review the structure of the CSF Core, Profile and Tiers to know how the framework organizes outcomes
* Reassess the organization regularly to maintain alignment with the target state



**2. Engage Leadership and Stakeholders** 

* Secure support from executives to ensure cybersecurity efforts are align with business goals
* Identify key stakeholders (IT, Security, operations, Legal, Compliance) who will participate in decisions
* Ensure leadership understands their role in defining risk appetite and approving major priorities 
* Align cybersecurity strategy with regulatory and business requirement for accountability 



**3. Assess the Current State** 

* Evaluate existing security policies, technical controls and operational practices
* Identify weakness such as outdated policies, missing safeguards or unmonitored systems
* Map current capabilities to NIST CSF outcomes to see what is working and what is lacking
* Document risks and gap that could impact business operations or compliance



**4. Defining the Target state** 

* Determine what level of security the organization needs based on the risk tolerance and legal obligations
* Set clear goals for each function 
* Decide which capabilities must be improved, introduced or completely redesigned
* Establish a desired cybersecurity posture that supports business continuity and long-term resilience 



**5. Develop a Roadmap** 

* Prioritize gaps based on risk severity, likelihood and business impact
* Build a structured improvement plan that outlines which tasks need to be completed first
* Include timelines, milestones, responsible teams and required resources
* Make sure the roadmap is realistic and tied to organization priorities 



**6. Implement Controls and Process**

* Deploy security control such as MFA, access management, data protection, patching and secure configurations
* Update or create policies 
* Improve technical safeguarding like logging, monitoring and endpoint protection 
* strengthen organizational practice such as vendor risk management and incident response readiness



**7. Monitor and Measure Progress** 

* Track progress using metrics such as control implementation rate, incident response time and MFA adoption 
* Review detection alerts, logs and incident report to ensure controls are actually effective
* Adjust the cybersecurity plan new threats, technologies or business changes arise
* Reassess the organization regularly to maintain alignment with the target state 



**8. Conduct Ongoing Training and Awareness** 

* Train employees on identifying phishing, social engineering and suspicious behaviour
* Provide regular updates on new threats and safe practices
* Promote a security-first culture where staff understand their role in protecting the organization 
* Include role-specific training for IT, HR, Finance and executive to reduce human-related risks
